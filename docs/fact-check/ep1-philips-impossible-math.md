## Fragment-by-Fragment Verification

### Fragment #1 — John 6:5 ✅ PASS (minor wording difference)

| | Text |
|---|---|
| **Narrative** | "When Jesus looked up and saw a great crowd coming toward him, he said to Philip, 'Where shall we buy bread for these people to eat?'" |
| **WEB** | "Jesus therefore lifting up his eyes, and seeing that a great multitude was coming to him, said to Philip, 'Where are we to buy bread, that these may eat?'" |

**Verdict:** The narrative uses NIV-style phrasing ("looked up", "great crowd", "Where shall we buy bread for these people to eat") rather than WEB phrasing ("lifting up his eyes", "great multitude", "Where are we to buy bread, that these may eat"). The content and meaning are accurate. The verse reference is correct — Jesus does ask Philip this question in John 6:5.

**⚠️ NOTE:** The quoted text matches the NIV, not the WEB. If WEB fidelity is required, the wording should be updated.

### Fragment #4 — John 6:7 ✅ PASS (wording difference)

| | Text |
|---|---|
| **Narrative** | "It would take more than half a year's wages to buy enough bread for each one to have a bite!" |
| **WEB** | "Two hundred denarii worth of bread is not sufficient for them, that every one of them may receive a little." |

**Verdict:** The narrative quotes the NIV rendering ("half a year's wages") rather than the WEB, which preserves the literal "two hundred denarii." However, the narrative prose correctly uses "two hundred denarii" throughout the story text itself. The 200 denarii figure is biblically accurate. Verse reference correct.

**On the 200 denarii figure:** ✅ Confirmed. The WEB footnote on Mark 6:37 notes "200 denarii was about 7 or 8 months wages for an agricultural laborer," which aligns with the narrative's characterization. The narrative's prose says "eight months' wages" — this is within the WEB's own footnote range of 7-8 months.

### Fragment #5 — Mark 6:37 ✅ PASS

| | Text |
|---|---|
| **Narrative** | "You give them something to eat." |
| **WEB** | "You give them something to eat." |

**Verdict:** Exact match with the WEB text of Mark 6:37. Verse reference correct.

**On mixing Mark with John:** This fragment is correctly attributed to Mark 6:37, not John. The Feeding of the 5000 is recorded in all four Gospels (Matthew 14, Mark 6, Luke 9, John 6). The command "You give them something to eat" appears in Mark's account but not in John's. Mixing synoptic sources for a harmonized narrative is standard practice in biblical storytelling and scholarship. The narrative's own summary acknowledges this: "This episode follows John 6:1-14 faithfully, with supplementary details from Mark 6:35-44." **This is acceptable.**

| **WEB** | "There is a boy here who has five barley loaves and two fish, but what are these among so many?" |

**Verdict:** Near-exact match. Minor differences: narrative says "two small fish" and "what are they" vs. WEB's "two fish" and "what are these." The word "small" does not appear in the WEB for John 6:9 (it appears in some other translations). The word "barley" is confirmed present in the WEB.

**On "barley loaves":** ✅ Confirmed. The WEB uses "barley loaves" in both John 6:9 and John 6:13. Barley bread was the cheaper grain, consistent with the narrative's description of "the poor man's grain."

**⚠️ MINOR:** The word "small" before "fish" is not in the WEB text of John 6:9. Consider removing it for accuracy, or noting it comes from other translations (e.g., NIV uses "small fish").

**⚠️ NOTE:** The WEB makes clear the distribution chain was Jesus → disciples → crowd. The narrative's quoted fragment simplifies this, but the story text itself correctly shows the disciples distributing.

## Additional Checks

### 200 Denarii Figure (John 6:7) ✅ CONFIRMED

The WEB text of John 6:7 explicitly states "Two hundred denarii." The WEB footnote on Mark 6:37 confirms this equals approximately 7-8 months' wages for an agricultural laborer. The narrative's use of "eight months' wages" falls within this range.

### "Barley Loaves" (John 6:9) ✅ CONFIRMED

The WEB uses "barley loaves" (Greek: κρίθινος/krithinos) in both John 6:9 and 6:13. This is accurate.

### Mixing Mark and John Sources ✅ ACCEPTABLE

Two fragments (#5 and #8) are from Mark rather than John:
- **Fragment #5** (Mark 6:37): "You give them something to eat" — not in John's account
- **Fragment #8** (Mark 6:40): Groups of hundreds and fifties — not in John's account

Both are clearly labeled with their Mark references. The narrative discloses this harmonization in its summary: "This episode follows John 6:1-14 faithfully, with supplementary details from Mark 6:35-44." All four Gospels record this miracle (Matt 14:13-21, Mark 6:30-44, Luke 9:10-17, John 6:1-14), and harmonizing parallel accounts is standard practice.

### Deuteronomy 18:15 → John 6:14 Connection ✅ CONFIRMED

Deuteronomy 18:15 (WEB): "Yahweh your God will raise up to you a prophet from among you, of your brothers, like me."

John 6:14 (WEB): "This is truly the prophet who comes into the world."

The definite article "the prophet" in John 6:14 is a direct reference to the Deuteronomy 18:15 prophecy. This is universally recognized in biblical scholarship. Acts 3:22-23 and Acts 7:37 explicitly connect this prophecy to Jesus.
